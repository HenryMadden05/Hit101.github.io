Conclusions

Impact of Explicitness and Genre on Track Success

One of the more surprising findings was that explicitness has little bearing on a track's popularity. Explicit songs have grown significantly more common over the last several decades and vary in prevalence by genre, yet this shift has not translated into a measurable advantage or disadvantage in terms of success. This suggests that audience reception of explicit content is largely genre-dependent, and that artists and labels need not treat explicitness as a strategic lever for broader appeal.

Era, Platform Growth, and Popularity Scores

Post-2010 tracks dominate the upper popularity range, while songs from the 1950s through the 1990s cluster at lower scores despite their cultural significance. This pattern likely reflects Spotify's own growth trajectory and its algorithmic tendency to surface content with active listener engagement. Furthermore, no single era dramatically outperforms others in average popularity, suggesting the platform distributes moderate success broadly. Notably, breaking into a mid-sized audience appears to have a larger impact on a track's popularity score than subsequent growth, pointing to platform visibility and established fanbase size as the most meaningful drivers of Spotify's metric.

Track Length, Title Words, and Release Format

Among the content-level features examined, track length showed the clearest relationship with popularity. Songs in the 3–5 minute range performed best, hovering around a popularity score of 55, suggesting that moderate length strikes the right balance between developing song structure and maintaining listener engagement. In contrast, word choice in song titles showed no meaningful correlation with average popularity, indicating that artists should not rely on commonly used title words as a strategy for discoverability. Similarly, release format — whether a single or album track — did not emerge as a strong predictor, though singles have become increasingly prominent in recent years.

Key Insights for the Music Industry

Our analysis offers several practical takeaways for artists, labels, and platform strategists:

01
Focus on platform visibility over content formulas

Since discoverability is driven more by fanbase size and platform placement than by explicitness, title wording, or release format, investment in promotion and playlist placement is likely more impactful than optimizing song content alone.

02
Target the 3–5 minute sweet spot

Track length is one of the few content features with a measurable relationship to popularity, making it a worthwhile consideration during production.

03
Account for recency bias in popularity metrics

Spotify's popularity score favors recent engagement, meaning older tracks may be undervalued despite their cultural weight. Strategies for re-promoting catalog music could unlock untapped visibility.

04
Tailor explicit content decisions by genre

Since explicitness varies significantly across genres and does not universally impact success, decisions around content should be guided by genre norms and target audience expectations.

Future Questions and Analysis

While this analysis surfaces meaningful patterns, several avenues remain open for deeper exploration:

Streaming counts and playlist placement

Incorporating raw streaming data and playlist inclusion metrics would allow for a more direct measure of reach, moving beyond Spotify's composite popularity score.

Listener demographics by region

Geographic breakdowns of listening behavior could reveal whether popularity drivers differ across markets, informing region-specific release and promotion strategies.

Longitudinal artist-level analysis

Examining how individual artists' popularity scores evolve over time — particularly around album releases, viral moments, or playlist features — could isolate the impact of specific events on long-term discoverability.

Impact of social media and algorithmic promotion

As short-form video platforms increasingly drive music discovery, future research could examine how external digital promotion correlates with Spotify popularity gains.

While no single feature guarantees a song's success, this analysis demonstrates that platform dynamics, release timing, and moderate track length are among the most consistent indicators of popularity on Spotify. As the music landscape continues to evolve, continued data-driven research will be essential for artists and industry stakeholders seeking to navigate an increasingly complex and algorithm-driven ecosystem.
